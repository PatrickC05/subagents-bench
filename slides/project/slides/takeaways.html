Takeaways and next steps

What we learned

  • Small subagents didn’t add accuracy, and on bug fixing alone doing it yourself is faster and cheaper
  • The wait is the big model’s thinking, not its tools
  • With mixed requests, subagents keep the agent available: 81% vs 56% of questions answered, about 5× faster

Next

  • Accurate timing: only 21–38% of bugs got an estimate
  • Steering and priorities: urgent bugs, changed plans
  • More rounds; workloads with slow commands; a faster orchestrator

Caveats: 4–12 sessions per condition; Sail latency varies; amd64 emulation on one Mac; manager subagents broke passing tests in 5 of 16 bugs-only sessions

To sum up. Small subagents didn’t buy accuracy, and when the user only sends bugs, doing the work yourself is faster and cheaper, because the waiting comes from the big model’s thinking. But when the user also asks questions and checks status, background subagents keep the agent available: far more questions answered, about five times faster, at the same fix rate. Next we want to measure timing, since agents rarely give estimates and miss them, and steering, plus more rounds and harder workloads. The caveats are small samples, provider latency, and emulation.
